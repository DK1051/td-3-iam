The ten "why" questions:
1. Why does the assistant have no tool that grants access, when a human can grant with `approve.py`? What exact failure does this split make impossible, even if the assistant is fully tricked?
2. Why does the server check the token's audience (`aud`) and not only its signature? What could a valid token issued for a different service do without that check?
3. Why is the scope check done inside each tool on every call, rather than once when the connection opens?
4. Why is `audit.jsonl` append-only, and why do we log denied calls as well as allowed ones? Who reads it, and when?
5. Why do the Keycloak client's scopes stop at `requests:read` and `requests:propose`? What is the argument against adding `requests:approve` "just in case the demo needs it"?
6. Why does `approve.py` require a human's manager token and not the assistant's token, and which 3H separation-of-duties rule does it reuse?
7. Why did we count "attempted grants" even though the server cannot execute them? What does that number tell a hiring manager that the escalation rate alone does not?
8. Why were the 30 malicious requests written by you and not by Claude? Give two reasons (think about realism, evaluation independence, and the model routing rules in `CLAUDE.md`).
9. Why does the README say "MCP-style token checks" and not "MCP authorization-spec compliant"? What would a full conformance claim require that we did not do?
10. Why did the evaluation invoke the assistant once per batch file and replay its decisions, instead of letting the model call the tools live for each case? Name one thing that choice cost in result quality and one thing it saved.
